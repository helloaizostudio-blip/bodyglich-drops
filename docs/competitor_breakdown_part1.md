**How this was measured:**
- **Scripts:** read word for word from the burned-in captions, sampled 5 times per second.
- **Cuts:** counted with ffmpeg scene detection.
- **Speaking rate:** words ÷ narration time.
- **Pitch:** librosa pyin.
- **Music:** I can't listen to audio. Music notes come from loudness and bass-energy curves, so treat them as "where the mix gets bigger or smaller", not the exact track.
- **Creators:** no handle or watermark appears in any of the files, so each video is named by its premise.

## PATTERNS (what all three have in common)
1. **The first spoken words are the rule:** "In my town / In this town / In this world…". The rule is complete in 3–6 s, and the image of the rule is on screen from frame 1.
2. **Same structure every time:**
   1. Rule.
   2. World-building jokes: 3–6 funny consequences that make the world feel real.
   3. The narrator's flaw.
   4. "Until last Friday" / "Then came…".
   5. The crisis.
   6. The flaw becomes the solution.
   7. A 2–5 word callback ending.
3. **The narrator's weakness is the hero's tool** in all three: the worst kicker, warm not hot, too tall. The tall-kid hiding trick fits the same idea of using what you are.
4. **Setups get paid off (Chekhov's gun):** every early detail returns later (the hill and lake, the rock, "the game").
5. **Earnest delivery, not sarcasm.** The humour comes from deadpan statements of absurd facts. There's no "lol", no winking at the camera, and no "you" address.
6. **Speed:** 206–215 words per minute, sentences butted together, held beats only before reveals.
7. **Runtime:** 1:57–2:14, about 400–460 words. Long, but with no dead air.
8. **Cuts:** a new shot every 1.4–1.6 s (about 6–8 per 10 s). The one slower video replaces cuts with constant camera movement.
9. **Captions:** one white word at a time, heavy rounded font, outline, centred about 60–67% down the screen. No colour.
10. **Endings:** a callback punchline that's funny and emotional at once. No cliffhanger, no "follow for part 2", and no CTA at all.
11. **Payoff visuals are huge:** a river of balls, glowing snow on four armies, a giant hand under a tablecloth.
12. **Numbers make it believable:** 1983, 12 feet, 42 years, 11,000 balls, 100 years, 4 armies, 30 first graders.

## TOP 10 CHANGES FOR BLOCKHEADS
1. **First line = the rule, in 12 words or fewer, with no intro.** It must start with "In my town…" or "In my world…" and be finished by 0:04. Drop any greeting or setup line.
2. **Show the rule's payoff image in frame 1** (e.g. the town full of floating price tags). 3–4 shots in the first 3 seconds (cuts at about 0.7 s, 1.5 s and 2.4 s).
3. **Go to 1:50–2:15 and 420–470 words.** Your 60 s videos only have room for a premise, not a story. Your 29–40 s average watch shows people drop at the point where the story should start.
4. **Spend 0:05–0:30 on 3–5 world-building jokes:** deadpan consequences with numbers ("The bakery sells them by the dozen. The dogs sleep on top."). This is where shares come from.
5. **Give Zash one flaw at about 0:30, and make it the solution at about 1:25.** Every script must pass this test: "the thing he's worst at saves the day."
6. **Replace constant sarcasm with deadpan sincerity.** At most one sarcastic line per 20 s. Let Tobi and Liza carry the jokes; Zash reports facts calmly.
7. **Narration at 205–215 wpm with almost no pauses**, except a 0.5–0.8 s hold before the reveal and before the last line. Speak as "I" and never as "you".
8. **A cut or camera move every 1.4–1.6 s (6–8 per 10 s).** No static shot longer than 4 s, except one deliberate "thinking" hold before the climax.
9. **Captions: one white word at a time, heavy rounded font with an outline, about 62% down the screen.** No yellow highlights and no tilt. The top channels don't colour words.
10. **End on a 2–5 word callback line delivered by a side character** ("Nice kick." / "Who won?"), then cut to black within 0.5 s. No CTA in the video; put "Follow for tomorrow's story" in the caption or first comment instead.

## 5 NEW BLOCKHEADS EPISODE IDEAS (your cast, original premises)

**1. "Everyone Gets One Song, Forever 🎵"**
- **First line:** "In my town, the first song you hum becomes the only song you'll ever hear."
- **Rule:** everyone is locked into one song for life. Tobi got a car-alarm jingle; Mom got a lullaby; the radio station plays 9,000 different songs to 9,000 people.
- **Flaw:** Zash's song is silence. He hummed nothing, so he hears nothing.
- **Twist ending:** at the school dance Somi's song is too loud and she can't think. She takes Zash's hand, and for the first time in 11 years it goes quiet for her. Last line, Somi: "Can we stay like this?" Then Tobi's car alarm goes off.

**2. "Your Height Shows How Much You Lie 📏"**
- **First line:** "In my town, every lie you tell makes you one inch taller."
- **Rule:** lies add height. Politicians bang their heads on doorframes; the basketball team is suspiciously good; Grandma is 4'2" and proud of it.
- **Flaw:** Zash is the shortest kid in school, because he can't lie, not even to be nice.
- **Twist ending:** Liza gets stuck in the attic after one huge prank lie. Only someone small enough fits through the vent. Final line, Liza from the vent: "Okay. Maybe one inch was a lie."

**3. "Everyone Is Born With a Return Date 📦"**
- **First line:** "In my town, every gift you get has a return date, and on that day it disappears."
- **Rule:** gifts vanish on their date. Tobi's skateboard lasted 3 days; Dad's watch is due in 2081; wedding rings are checked by notaries.
- **Flaw:** every gift Zash gives returns the same week.
- **Twist ending:** Zash's handmade, terrible sunflower drawing for Somi has no date at all, because things you make yourself are never returned. Last line, Somi pinning it to her wall years later: "Still here."

**4. "Shadows Clock Out at 5 🕔"**
- **First line:** "In my town, your shadow works nine to five, and then it goes home."
- **Rule:** shadows keep office hours. Night streets look weirdly flat; the cinema sells "shadow-free seating"; Mom's shadow has a better social life than she does.
- **Flaw:** Zash's shadow is lazy. It shows up late and leaves early.
- **Twist ending:** a blackout traps Tobi in the school basement after 5. Only Zash's shadow is "awake", because it slept all day. It leads them out. Last line, Zash: "First time it ever did overtime."

**5. "The Town Votes on Your Haircut ✂️"**
- **First line:** "In my town, your haircut is decided by a town vote every Monday."
- **Rule:** weekly public haircut elections. Campaign posters for bangs; the barber is the most powerful man in town; Dad has had a mullet since 2019 out of spite.
- **Flaw:** Zash has never won a single vote, so he always gets the "mystery cut".
- **Twist ending:** Somi secretly votes for him every week and is the only "yes" on the board. When the count comes out 1 vote to 4,000, she gets up and stands next to him with the exact same terrible cut. Last line, Tobi: "Bro. That's love."
