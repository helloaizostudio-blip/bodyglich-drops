## How this changes the part 1 advice

**There are two winning formats, not one:**

| | Long story (part 1 videos) | Short twist (A, B) | Horror twist (C) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Length | 1:57–2:14 | 0:36–0:37 | 1:28 |
| Words per minute | 206–215 | **308–316** | 273 |
| Average shot | 1.4–1.6 s | **0.9–1.0 s** | 1.1 s |
| Words on screen | 1 | **2–4, in 2 lines** | 2–3, in 2 lines |
| Coloured key word | none | **green, always** | colour by meaning |
| Caption position | about 62% down | **upper third** | about 70% down |
| Twist arrives at | about 1:20 | **0:08** | 0:40 |
| Ending | 2–5 word callback | **2–5 word punchline that flips the hook** | sting aimed at the viewer |

**What all six share:**
- The hook says the rule or the deal in the first 2–3 s, with the payoff image already on screen.
- Escalation in 3–4 visible steps.
- An early detail is called back in the last line.
- The last line is 1–5 words.
- No plain "follow me" anywhere. The only CTA (Video C) is told as part of the story.

**Correction to part 1:** I said "no coloured caption words". That's true for the long-story style only. The short and horror styles **do** colour exactly one key word per caption. So keep one highlighted word per caption, in one consistent colour (or colour by meaning, like Video C), but drop the tilt, put 2–4 words on screen at once, and move the captions to the upper third.

### What I'd do with Blockheads now
Your viewers leave at 29–40 s. **The short format fits that today.** A 36–40 s episode with that same watch time means most viewers finish it. That's the strongest signal you can send the algorithm.

1. **Main format: 35–40 s "short twist" episodes.**
   - About 300 wpm.
   - A cut every 0.9–1.0 s.
   - The twist by 0:08.
   - A 1–3 word last line that flips the first line.
2. **Once a week: a 1:30–2:00 story** (part 1 style, or horror-twist style) to build fans.
3. **Captions:** 2 lines, 2–4 words, one bold key word in a single brand colour (yellow for us), upper third, sound effects as their own word (CLINK, THUMP).
4. **If you add a CTA, make it part of the story:** a last line that uses the world's own rule ("In my world, people who don't follow get the mystery haircut. Just saying."). Use it at most once every few episodes.
5. **Zash's look must read in a thumbnail.** Both short videos use one loud signature design (green spikes and stripes). Zash's mustard hoodie works; keep it on him in every episode.

## 5 SHORT-FORMAT BLOCKHEADS IDEAS (35–40 s, your cast, original)

**1. "Saying 'I'm Fine' Makes It Rain on You 🌧️"**
- **First line:** "In my world, every time you say 'I'm fine', it rains on your head."

**2. "Every Lie Rings a Bell 🔔"**
- **First line:** "In my world, every time you lie, a bell rings over your head."
- **Escalation:** Liza's room sounds like a church. Dad rings softly every morning ("five minutes"). Zash asks Somi if she likes his drawing: "It's okay." DING. DING. DING.

**3. "Your Plants Grow When You're Happy 🌱"**

**4. "Liza Bet Me I Couldn't Stay Silent for a Week 🤫"**

- **Last line:** at day 7 Somi says "I like you." Zash opens his mouth. Liza: "Pay up."

**5. "Your Height Drops When You're Embarrassed 📉"**
- **First line:** "In my world, every time you get embarrassed, you shrink an inch."

- **Last line:** Somi picks him up, now pocket-sized: "Finally, a guy my height."

## TEMPLATE: 37-second short-twist episode (about 190 words at 305 wpm)

| 0:15–0:24 | Escalation montage | 3 rapid visual gags, about 1 s each, rising |
| 0:24–0:30 | "Then came the ___" | The big final situation |
| 0:30–0:35 | Payoff setup | A reveal or a question asked out loud |
| 0:35–0:37 | Punchline | 1–3 words that flip the first line. Cut to black. |

- **Shots:** one every 0.9–1.0 s. Push-ins, close-ups on reactions, inserts for objects. Nothing static over 2 s.

- **Sound:** a light bed throughout. One repeating sound effect as the joke engine (clink, ding, thump).
